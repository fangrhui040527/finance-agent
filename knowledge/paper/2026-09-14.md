## Mistakes

**One, and it is in the record rather than in tonight's decision.**

Every Malaysian "unexplained share" quoted on the 2026-09-10 through 2026-09-13 paper pages was computed against a proxy that gave a bank a beta of 0.12. The pages were not wrong about the arithmetic and were explicit that the proxy was suspect from 09-11 onward — the 09-12 page called the resulting decomposition "close to worthless" in those words. But the 09-10 and 09-11 pages used the figures as though they meant something about the companies. **They did not.** The correction is recorded here rather than back-edited into those pages.

**The near-miss worth naming.** `sweep --due` reported `us_close` as owed at 22:37 UTC, 1h22m past its cron. On 2026-09-14 I dispatched a slot in that state and produced a duplicate run, because I treated an observed 5h43m delay maximum as a bound. Tonight the same reading produced the opposite action: no dispatch, and the scheduled run arrived at **23:51:43 UTC, +2h36m**. The rule that changed is small and worth keeping — *the delay distribution says when a run is missing; the cron only says when it was due.*

**Not a mistake, but worth flagging as a defect this book depends on.** The printed decomposition omits the regression intercept (`engines/attribution/decompose.py:197`), so the components have never summed to the return. Genting's fitted drift is −0.24pp/day, twice its entire printed idiosyncratic component. Any reading of "how much was company-specific" on any page in this journal is off by that name's alpha.
