1. A multiple alone is a number, not a finding
Price to earnings of 18 is neither cheap nor expensive until it is placed. Against the same company's own history it may be at the tenth or the ninetieth percentile. Against peers it may be a premium or a discount. Against expected growth at the company's return on capital it may be justified or not. Only all three together make a statement.

2. Own history
The percentile of today's multiple in the company's own record over several years, with the record's low and high, is the first context and the one this system reports first, because it needs no peer set and no forecast. Snapshots accumulate in the fact book with every collection run, so the record deepens over time. A percentile computed on a short history is labelled with its length.

3. Peers
The peer band is the median and range of the same multiple across companies that compete with or are classified alongside the name, on the same market, on the same date. A peer set of one is refused; two is thin and said to be thin. Peers come from the knowledge graph's competition and classification edges, so the set is inspectable and the evidence for each membership is a cited edge.

4. Growth-adjusted
Dividing the multiple by expected growth, or comparing implied growth from a reverse DCF with the base rate, adjusts for the fact that a faster-growing business deserves a higher multiple at the same return on capital. The adjustment is only as good as the growth estimate, which is why the reverse DCF's implied growth is reported next to it.

5. Which multiple
Earnings multiples for stable earners; enterprise value to EBITDA or EBIT where capital structures differ; price to book for banks; enterprise value to gross profit for software with no margin history; FFO for REITs; never a trailing earnings multiple for a cyclical near a peak. The archetype table in the valuation agent picks the method and states the forbidden one.

6. References
Liu, Nissim and Thomas for the evidence on which multiples predict prices best; Damodaran for the relative-valuation framework.
