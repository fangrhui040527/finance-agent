1. The formula and its trap
The terminal value at the end of the explicit forecast is the next year's free cash flow divided by the discount rate minus the terminal growth rate. As the growth rate approaches the discount rate the denominator approaches zero and the value approaches infinity, so a small change in terminal growth moves the answer a great deal. This is where optimistic models hide their optimism.

2. The ceiling
A business cannot grow faster than the economy it operates in forever, or it becomes the economy. The terminal growth rate is therefore bounded above by long-run nominal growth, and a common practical ceiling is the risk-free rate, on the argument that the risk-free rate embeds expected nominal growth. Models built at pandemic-era peaks with 6 to 7 percent terminal growth for consumer internet names were the textbook violation. This system's sanity check refuses any scenario whose terminal growth exceeds the risk-free rate or the long-run nominal growth in the cost-of-capital table.

3. Reinvestment in perpetuity
Terminal growth also needs terminal reinvestment. A perpetuity growing at 3 percent with a return on capital of 10 percent must reinvest 30 percent of its operating profit after tax to sustain that growth. A terminal cash flow that assumes growth with no reinvestment is another free lunch.

4. Report the share
The terminal value's share of enterprise value is a diagnostic, not a footnote. Above three quarters, the explicit forecast is doing little work and the valuation is a statement about the perpetuity assumptions. The scenario engine reports the share on every result and flags a share above 75 percent as material.

5. Cross-check with an exit multiple
Converting the terminal value into an implied multiple of terminal-year earnings or EBITDA, and comparing it with where mature businesses of the kind trade, catches perpetuity assumptions that look reasonable as a growth rate and absurd as a multiple. The reverse also holds: an exit multiple chosen without stating its implied growth is a hidden assumption.
