1. Hospitals: beds, occupancy, revenue intensity
A hospital group's revenue is beds times occupancy times revenue per admission, and its margin depends on case mix and staffing cost. Growth is new capacity, which takes years and capital, or acquisitions. Regulatory exposure is price control on private healthcare and licensing of facilities; foreign operations carry currency and country risk that the group's home listing does not show. Enterprise value to EBITDA against peers with the same geographic mix is the working multiple; a DCF works if the capacity plan is explicit.

2. Gaming: the licence is the asset
A casino operator's value rests on the licence, its term and its renewal terms, on the tax rate applied to gaming revenue, and on the visitor economy around it. Non-gaming revenue and the hotel and leisure assets are valued separately. Regulatory and tax changes arrive by budget announcement and can reprice the business in a day; they are the scheduled events to watch. Leverage taken to build capacity before a licence renewal is the covenant-cliff pattern in the failure library.

3. Holding companies: sum of the parts with a discount
A holding company is valued by valuing each business it owns with the method appropriate to that business, summing, subtracting net debt at the holding level, and applying a discount for the costs, the control structure and the tax leakage between the parts. The discount is stated, not implied; a consolidated multiple on a conglomerate hides the fact that its parts deserve different multiples.

4. Where the pieces come from
Segment reporting in the annual report is the source for the parts; a company that reorganises its segments frequently is making the comparison harder, and segment reorganisation is itself a failure-library pattern. Listed subsidiaries give a market value for some parts directly.

5. In this system
The valuation agent's table sets hospitals to enterprise value multiples against a matched peer set, gaming to a licence-and-capacity view with the tax rate as an explicit input, and holding companies to a sum of parts with a stated discount, marking consolidated multiples not applicable.
