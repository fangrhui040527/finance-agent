1. The measured error rate
Green, Hand and Zhang examined published sell-side DCF models and found a median of three theory or execution errors and four questionable economic judgements per model, and that correcting a single major error moved the target price by between 2 and 14 percent. The models were produced by professionals with resources this system does not have. The lesson is not that DCF is useless; it is that a DCF needs mechanical checks that do not depend on the modeller's mood.

2. The catalogue
Growth rates inconsistent with the industry, the market or the company's own history. Terminal growth above nominal GDP or the risk-free rate. Working capital ignored. The wrong discount rate for the cash flow definition. Margins projected above anything the business has achieved, without a stated reason. Discount rates below the risk-free rate plus a premium. Double counting: cash added back to equity value while its interest income stays in the cash flows. Mid-year versus end-year discounting applied inconsistently. Share counts that ignore dilution from options and convertibles.

3. The checks, as this system runs them
Terminal growth above the risk-free rate or the table's long-run growth: fatal, the scenario is refused. Terminal value above 75 percent of enterprise value: material. Projected margin above the five-year historical maximum: material, unless the note supplies a reason. Projected growth more than twice the historical rate: material. Working capital set to zero while history shows it non-zero: minor. Discount rate below the risk-free rate plus half the equity premium, or cost of equity below cost of debt: fatal. A fatal flag removes the scenario from the range and the output says why.

4. What the checks cannot catch
Whether the story behind the base case is true. A model can be internally consistent and wrong about the world. The checks guarantee that the range is built from assumptions that do not contradict arithmetic or the company's own record; the thesis and its breakers are where the story is tested, and the red team's analogues are where its resemblance to past failures is surfaced.

5. Use comparables alongside
No institutional process relies on a DCF alone. The peer band and the own-history percentile are the cross-check; when the DCF range and the multiples disagree by a wide margin, one of them carries an assumption that needs a second look.

6. References
