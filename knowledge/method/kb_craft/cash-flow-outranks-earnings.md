1. The hardest number to fake
Cash from operations is the sum of money that actually arrived from customers minus money that actually left for suppliers, staff and tax. It can be manipulated at the margin, by delaying payables or factoring receivables, but not for long and not without leaving traces in working capital. Net income can be moved by a change in an estimate. That asymmetry is why cash flow outranks earnings in every check this system runs.

2. Accruals: the gap, and what it predicts
Accruals are net income minus cash from operations, usually scaled by average total assets. Sloan's 1996 finding, replicated many times since, is that firms with high accruals go on to report lower earnings and lower returns than firms with low accruals, because the accrual component of earnings is less persistent than the cash component. A large positive accrual ratio is not proof of anything, but it is the single best-documented warning sign in the accounting literature.

3. The pattern to recognise
Earnings rising while operating cash flow falls, for more than one year, with receivables or inventory growing faster than revenue. A company reporting record profit and negative operating cash flow for the third year is either investing heavily in working capital for a reason it can explain, or it is recognising revenue it has not collected. The question to ask is which, and the answer must come from the notes and the receivables ageing, not from the narrative.

4. Free cash flow and what it is for
Free cash flow is cash from operations minus capital expenditure. It is what remains to pay dividends, buy back shares, repay debt or acquire. A business whose free cash flow is persistently below its reported profit is spending its earnings on staying in place. A business whose free cash flow exceeds profit for years is either under-investing or has a genuinely light model; the capex-to-depreciation ratio tells which.

5. In this system
A1's earnings-quality check flags a net income to operating cash flow gap above 30 percent. The quality engine computes the Sloan accrual ratio and the Beneish indices when the statement lines are stored, and says how many of the inputs it has rather than reporting a score built on missing pieces.
