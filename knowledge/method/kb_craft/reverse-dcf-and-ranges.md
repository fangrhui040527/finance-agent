1. Turning the model around
A forward DCF asks: given my assumptions, what is the business worth? A reverse DCF asks: given the price, what must the business deliver? Holding the discount rate fixed and solving for the growth rate that makes the present value equal the price gives the growth the market is already requiring. That number can be compared with the company's history, its peers and the base rate for businesses of its size. The question becomes whether the requirement is plausible, which is a much easier question than what the business is worth.

2. What the implied number is, and is not
It is a statement about the price, not an estimate of value. A stock requiring 25 percent annual earnings growth for ten years is not thereby overvalued; it is a stock whose owners are betting on an outcome that has a known base rate. The base rate for a large company sustaining 25 percent growth for a decade is small, and that is the finding to record.

3. Why a range and never a point
A point target pretends to a precision no input possesses. The discount rate is uncertain by a percentage point or two; the terminal growth by a half point; the margins by several points. Propagating those uncertainties through the model produces a range, and the range is the true output. Bear, base and bull scenarios make the range explicit and tie each end to a stated set of assumptions. A memo that says 40 to 62 with the assumptions for each end is more useful, and more honest, than one that says 51.

4. What the range is for
It is not a trade signal. It says where the current price sits relative to what the analyst can defend, and which assumptions would have to hold for the price to be right. When the price is inside the range, the finding is that the market and the analyst broadly agree and the position is a bet on the drivers, not on a mispricing. When it is outside, the finding is a disagreement to investigate, starting with the possibility that the analyst is wrong.

5. In this system
The valuation agent's reverse DCF reports the implied growth with the caveat that it is what the price requires. The scenario engine emits bear-to-bull ranges only, and refuses any scenario whose terminal growth exceeds the risk-free rate or whose margins exceed the business's own historical maximum without a stated reason.
