1. Definitions
Drawdown is the decline from the running peak to the current price, as a fraction of the peak. Maximum drawdown is the largest such decline over a window. Distance from the 52-week high is the same idea with a fixed window and is a common screen input, which is a reason to treat it with suspicion as a signal: what everyone watches is priced.

2. What it describes
The current drawdown says how far the price has fallen from its best; it is a description of the path. Combined with volatility it says whether the fall is large relative to the stock's normal dispersion. A 20 percent drawdown in a stock with 60 percent annualised volatility is an ordinary month; in a utility it is an event.

3. What it does not say
A large drawdown is not a buy signal and a new high is not a sell signal. Stocks in deep drawdowns include both recoveries and bankruptcies, and the distinction is in the fact book, not the chart. The system reports drawdown alongside the decomposition of the move so that the reader sees how much of the fall was the market.

4. Base rate
None measured. A claim such as "stocks 30 percent off their high recover within a year" has a base rate that depends on the period, the market and the reason, and this note makes no such claim.
