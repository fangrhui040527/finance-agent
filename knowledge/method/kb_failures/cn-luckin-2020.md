1. What the numbers showed beforehand
A coffee chain growing store count at an extraordinary rate, funded by successive equity raises, reporting per-store sales and average selling prices that an anonymous short report, based on thousands of hours of store footage and receipts, said were overstated by a wide margin. The reported unit economics improved faster than any comparable chain's ever had.

2. What happened
In April 2020 the company disclosed that its chief operating officer and others had fabricated transactions worth roughly RMB 2.2 billion in 2019. The shares fell about 80 percent in a day, the company was delisted from Nasdaq, and it settled with the SEC.

3. Pattern
Fabricated sales at the unit level, financed by a capital-raise treadmill that required the growth story to continue; and evidence available to anyone willing to count customers rather than read the filings.

4. The check in this system that would have caught it
The base-rate question: how many chains have improved unit economics this fast. The ratio of external financing to operating cash flow. And the discipline of treating a detailed, falsifiable short report as a hypothesis to test rather than an attack to dismiss.

5. References
The SEC's press release is public domain; the short report is the primary source for the pre-disclosure evidence.
