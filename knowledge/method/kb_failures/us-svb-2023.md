1. What the numbers showed beforehand
A bank whose deposits had tripled in two years, drawn from a single sector of venture-backed technology companies, and whose assets had been invested in long-dated government-backed bonds bought at low yields. As rates rose through 2022 the market value of those bonds fell by an amount comparable to the bank's equity, disclosed in the annual report as unrealised losses on held-to-maturity securities. Most deposits were above the insured limit.

2. What happened
In March 2023 the bank sold part of its securities at a loss and announced a capital raise. Depositors, concentrated, connected to one another and uninsured, withdrew tens of billions in a day, and the bank was closed by regulators the next morning.

3. Pattern
Duration mismatch between long assets and demand liabilities; single-sector concentration on the funding side, which behaved like a single customer when it moved; and a disclosed loss that the equity market discounted until the deposit market did not.

4. The check in this system that would have caught it
Unrealised losses on the securities book against tangible equity; the share of uninsured deposits; the concentration of the deposit base by sector. For a bank the balance sheet is the business, and the failure library's lesson is that the numbers were in the annual report a year early.

5. References
The Federal Reserve's own review is public domain and unusually candid; the 2022 annual report carries the figures.
