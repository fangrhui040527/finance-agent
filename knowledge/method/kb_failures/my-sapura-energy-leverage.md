1. What the numbers showed beforehand
An oilfield services group built by acquisition during the high-oil-price years, with a fleet and a drilling business financed by borrowings sized to peak-cycle earnings. When oil prices fell from 2014 the order book and margins compressed while the debt did not; a large rights issue in 2018 bought time; the pandemic downcycle removed the rest of the margin.

2. What happened
By 2022 the group could not service its borrowings, disclosed impairments and losses that took shareholders' equity below the threshold, and was classified PN17. A restructuring of the debt followed with heavy dilution for equity holders.

3. Pattern
A cyclical business whose leverage was set on peak-cycle margins; a covenant and refinancing cliff that arrived with the trough; and a capital-raise treadmill that deferred but did not remove the problem.

4. The check in this system that would have caught it
Net debt against mid-cycle EBITDA rather than peak EBITDA; interest cover through the trough; the capex-to-depreciation ratio at the peak; and the valuation agent's archetype rule that a cyclical is valued on mid-cycle earnings power, never on a trailing multiple. The sanity check that refuses margins above the historical maximum also refuses the reverse: a base case that assumes the peak margin persists.
