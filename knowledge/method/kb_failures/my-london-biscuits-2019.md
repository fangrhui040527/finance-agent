1. What the numbers showed beforehand
A snack manufacturer whose receivables were large and slow relative to a consumer business selling through distributors, whose property, plant and equipment was large relative to revenue, and whose borrowings had grown to fund both. Reported profits were modest and positive; cash from operations was thin.

2. What happened
In 2019 the company defaulted on bank facilities, was classified PN17, and its auditors raised qualifications about receivables and asset values. Investigations followed and the company was delisted.

3. Pattern
A receivables run in a business where receivables should be short; a covenant and repayment cliff reached when banks stopped rolling facilities; and auditor qualifications as the public confirmation of what the ratios had shown.

4. The check in this system that would have caught it
Receivable days against the sector norm, asset turnover falling while borrowings rose, and interest cover. The comparison with peers is the discriminating check: receivables of many months in a snack business are anomalous even when profit is positive.
