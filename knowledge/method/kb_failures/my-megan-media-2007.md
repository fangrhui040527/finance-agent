1. What the numbers showed beforehand
A manufacturer of optical discs reporting rapid revenue growth, with receivables a very large share of total assets and a balance sheet that carried more borrowings than the operating scale seemed to require. Cash from operations was persistently weak relative to reported profit.

2. What happened
In 2007 the company defaulted on debt obligations, and the investigation that followed found that a substantial part of the reported trading and the receivables behind it were fictitious, and that borrowings had been taken against them. The company entered default, its directors faced enforcement action, and it was delisted the following year.

3. Pattern
Receivables run combined with fabricated sales, with leverage raised on the strength of the fictitious assets; the going-concern language arrived with the default, after the equity had already been lost.

4. The check in this system that would have caught it
Receivables as a share of total assets and receivable days against peers; net debt against operating cash flow rather than against reported EBITDA; the accrual ratio. The failure library's own lesson is that the ratio of receivables to revenue was the earliest signal and was public.
