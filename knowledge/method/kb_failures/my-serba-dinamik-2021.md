1. What the numbers showed beforehand
An oil and gas services group that grew revenue and profit rapidly through acquisitions and overseas contracts, funded by repeated bond issues and share placements. Receivables and contract assets grew with revenue; cash from operations lagged reported profit; the gap was financed by new capital. Analyst coverage was broadly positive on the growth.

2. What happened
In 2021 the auditor reported that it could not resolve issues concerning a large volume of transactions, counterparties and balances. The company responded by suing the auditor, which then resigned. A special independent review followed, the Securities Commission charged the chief executive and others, the company fell into PN17 status, and it was delisted in 2023.

3. Pattern
Growth funded on a capital-raise treadmill; receivables and contract assets running ahead of cash; transactions with counterparties that could not be verified; and the auditor dispute as the first unambiguous public signal. The company's reaction to the auditor, litigation rather than disclosure, was itself the tell.

4. The check in this system that would have caught it
Free cash flow persistently negative while profit rose; the ratio of cumulative external financing to cumulative operating cash flow; receivable days; and the auditor-change event kind. The red team's standing challenge on accounting applies whenever a thesis has not checked earnings against cash, which was the case for most of this company's coverage.
