1. What the numbers showed beforehand
A payments company whose reported profit came largely from third-party acquiring partners in Asia whose existence and volumes could not be independently confirmed; cash balances held in escrow accounts at foreign trustees rather than at the company's own banks; growth by acquisition of businesses whose price bore little relation to their disclosed scale. Cash conversion looked adequate only because the cash itself was the fiction.

2. What happened
After years of press reporting and a special investigation that could not verify the third-party business, the auditor refused in June 2020 to sign the accounts because EUR 1.9 billion of cash could not be confirmed. The company admitted the cash probably did not exist and filed for insolvency within days.

3. Pattern
Fabricated sales through unverifiable related counterparties; profit concentrated in a segment with no independent evidence; and a reaction to scrutiny, legal action against journalists and regulators, that resembled Serba Dinamik's response to its auditor.

4. The check in this system that would have caught it
Segment concentration of profit in a business with no verifiable footprint; cash held away from the company's own banks as a balance-sheet note; the accrual and acquisition-spend ratios. The library's lesson is that a company that attacks its critics rather than answering them has answered.
