1. What the numbers showed beforehand
A pre-revenue vehicle company that reached a market value above that of established manufacturers on the strength of announced partnerships, pre-orders that were not binding, and demonstrations of technology. There were no sales to fabricate; the fabricated quantity was the state of the product. Cash burn required continuous capital raising.

2. What happened
A short report in September 2020 alleged that a widely circulated demonstration video showed a prototype rolling downhill rather than driving. The founder resigned, partners renegotiated, the SEC charged the company and the founder, who was convicted in 2022. The company continued for several years on new capital before filing for bankruptcy in 2025.

3. Pattern
A capital-raise treadmill with no operating cash flow to stand on; claims about the product that stood in for sales; and going-concern risk that was structural from the first day, whatever the market capitalisation said.

4. The check in this system that would have caught it
The valuation agent refuses forward multiples on pre-profit names and requires a reverse DCF: what revenue and margin, by when, does the price require. For this company the answer required outcomes with no precedent in the industry. The base-rate question is the whole analysis.
