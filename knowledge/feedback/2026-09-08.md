**The finding of the night: the US market proxy was fetched mid-session and then
served all day, so the three US names on this page are four sessions stale — and
the cause is in the price cache, not the feed.**

`data/price_cache.db` holds a body for `SPY` stamped `fetched_on 2026-09-08`
whose last row is dated 2026-09-08 and is not a bar:

```

2026-09-04,772.010009765625,772.8699951171875,769.0,770.1900024414062,34015600.0
2026-09-08,772.010009765625,769.7000122070312,767.260009765625,767.2899780273438,2948758.0
```

The open is the previous day's open, repeated, and sits above its own high. The
bar parser drops it as an inverted bar, correctly. The cache then reported a hit
for every later read that UTC day, so the 22:37 `us_close` sweep — which would
have fetched the finished session — never asked. The volume, 2.9m against 34m
the session before, is the tell: this is a partial session, fetched around the
13:41 UTC sweep, hours before the US close.

its own parser rejects as a MISS (`core/market/cache.is_mid_session`), and the
pack marks any row whose name printed a session its proxy did not as

(`knowledge/pack.Move.mis_dated`). The second is the general form of yesterday's
Bursa blank-row finding; it caught this one on its first run, on a different

## Lessons proposed

- Derived data dated by when it was fetched, rather than by what it contains,
  will serve half a session as a whole one. The cache's own stated reason — "a
  daily bar cannot change until a new session prints" — is true only of a body
  fetched after that session closed, and nothing checked which kind it held.
- A parser that correctly rejects bad input, upstream of a cache that does not,
  produces the quietest possible failure: the data is dropped where it is
  noticed and the hit is reported where it is not.
- A fault found on one market is worth generalising before the next market
  finds it independently. Yesterday's Bursa blank-row finding and tonight's US
  mid-session finding are the same page-level defect — figures dated to a day
  they are not from — reached by two different routes, one day apart.
