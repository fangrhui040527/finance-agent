### The Bursa dating fault, mechanism found

Three pages have now been mis-dated. Previous pages recorded the effect and marked the cause unknown. It is no longer unknown.

`MYX:0820EA`, the FBM KLCI ETF used as the Malaysian market proxy (`core/market/feed.py:419`), is too thinly traded to date a session:

```
median daily volume, last 60 sessions      2,800 shares
        Maybank, same window          11,027,500 shares      3,937x
        SPY, same window              43,844,800 shares
sessions with zero volume, full history    164 of 1,231  (13.3%)
```

`knowledge/pack.py:112` takes `sorted(set(closes_i) & set(closes_m))` and `:120` takes its last element as the session to report. A proxy with no close on 2026-09-09 drops that date out of the intersection, so the "1 day" return for every Bursa name silently becomes 09-07 → 09-08. The page states `last session 2026-09-08` correctly; nothing states that this is not the page's day.

And the history is not stable between fetches, which is what made this hard to see:

| session | as fetched 2026-09-09 | as fetched 2026-09-10 |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-09-08 | **blank** | 1.8050 |
| 2026-09-09 | 1.8200 | **blank** |

A settled session's close was present in one fetch and absent in the next, in both directions. Every retrospective check finds the data correct, because by then it has healed.

**This is not a cache bug.** It is a proxy that does not trade. An instrument with 13% no-trade sessions cannot date the sessions of instruments that trade every day. The candidate remedy is the index itself (`^KLSE`) rather than an ETF tracking it; the ETF was chosen because "price feeds carry ETFs under the same symbol rules as any share, and an index symbol would need a rule of its own per source" (`core/market/feed.py:412`). That trade-off now has a measured cost. **I could not verify from this environment that `^KLSE` resolves** — Yahoo returns 403 through the sandbox proxy for every symbol, including ones that work on the runner — so the remedy is proposed, not tested.

Cost of the fault, measured on this page: the six Bursa names are reported on the wrong session; four of six have the wrong sign for 2026-09-09 (Tenaga, PCHEM, IHH, Press Metal); and the book's largest move of the day, Genting -3.98%, is reported as -1.95% and cannot be attributed at all.

### The GDELT cap: the question is answered, and the answer is no

Carried since 2026-09-08: *does the refusal rate fall over a full rotation, or was the 429-budget reading wrong?*

Measured over every recorded GDELT sweep:

```
pre-cap   (9 names asked per run, 26 runs with a failure)   87 of 234 refused   37%
post-cap  (3 names asked per run, 5 runs)                    9 of  15 refused   60%
```

The refusal rate did not fall. On this sample it rose. The sample is 15 requests and the difference is not significant on its own, but it is decisively inconsistent with the premise the cap was justified by: if GDELT handed out a per-run quota that nine requests exhausted, three requests would be refused rarely. They are refused more often than before.

**So the "quota gone after about five" reading was wrong**, and PR #59 justified a correct change with an incorrect mechanism. The change is still worth having — it stopped GDELT spending the sweep deadline on requests that were going to be refused, which is what starved the other sources — but it was never going to make the refusals stop, and the next person reading that PR would have expected it to.

A second thing the measurement exposed, not previously noticed: **the cap only ever binds `bursa_close`.** `_window` returns the whole list when `cap >= len(terms)`; `us_close` sweeps the three Nasdaq names, so the cap of 3 never applies to it. Every capped run in the record is a `bursa_close` run:

```

2026-09-08 22:37  us_close     3 asked, 2 refused, no deferral
2026-09-09 13:47  bursa_close  3 asked, 2 refused, deferred: Petronas Chemicals, IHH, Press Metal
2026-09-09 22:38  us_close     3 asked, 1 refused, no deferral
2026-09-10 13:39  bursa_close  3 asked, 2 refused, deferred: Tenaga, Petronas Chemicals, IHH
```

This is correct behaviour for the code as written, and it means the tiling guarantee applies to the six Malaysian names only. It also means Petronas Chemicals has been deferred by GDELT on two of the three capped runs while collecting nothing from Google News either.

## Lessons proposed

- A proxy must be at least as liquid as the things it explains. Liquidity is a
  precondition for a benchmark, not a quality of it, and nothing in this system
  checked it before using one.
- A change can be right and its justification wrong, and the justification is
  what the next person inherits. The GDELT cap improved the sweep and the
  mechanism written into its PR was false; only measuring after the change
  caught it.
- "It did not run" and "it has not run yet" are different findings, and a check
  that fires inside the delay window cannot tell them apart. Before reporting an
  absence, establish the distribution of the thing's arrival time.
- Three consecutive pages carried the same defect with the cause marked unknown,
  and the cause was two SQL queries away. Marking a cause unknown is honest; it
  is not a substitute for looking again the next night.
