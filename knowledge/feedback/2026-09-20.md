## Lessons proposed

- A delay band is a measurement of the days it was taken on. Ten no-ops at +1h20m were correct against a cron that ran two hours late; on a day when the same cron ran six hours late, +1h20m meant something else, and the rule had to be re-read against that day before it was applied.
- One row on the seventh day is a title, not a body. The first Petronas Chemicals row in six days names the company in a short-interest roundup for the week it fell nine percent; it says the position existed and nothing about why, and the collector has no source that could.
- The store's faults print themselves on the page. A *last EPS surprise* knowable on 2026-09-30 sits in NVIDIA's snapshot nine days before that date; the reader can see the point-in-time defect without opening the database, which is the right place for it to be seen.
