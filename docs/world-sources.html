The one rule this whole register hangs on

A source's tier decides what it is allowed to do. Nothing on a lower rung may ever overwrite something on a higher one.

The ladder is not a style guide. It is TrustTier in core/contracts/answer.py, and the comment beside it says a web result "may never overwrite a filing or a price series". Every row in every table below inherits a rung, and that rung decides whether the thing it says can be treated as evidence, as a pointer, or as colour.

0LEDGERYour own holdings and trades. Nothing outranks it and nothing external may correct it.
1MARKET_SERIESPrices, volumes, corporate actions. What actually happened.
2FILINGSWhat the company itself reported, with the date it became public.
3METHOD_KBThe craft library and official statistics. How to think, and the state of the world.
4TRANSCRIPTSWhat management said out loud. First-hand, but self-interested.
5CURATED_NEWSEdited reporting. The highest rung anything wired today reaches.
6WEB_SEARCHA pointer to evidence. Never the evidence.
How to read the tables

no key nothing to sign up for. free key a signup, then free. paid or licence-restricted — read the notes column before building on it.

T6

Web search — the bridge you can use today

The fastest way to get knowledge in, and the easiest way to quietly destroy the trust ladder. Used correctly it is a pointer, not a source.

A search result is not evidence. It is a claim that evidence exists somewhere. The correct shape is: search finds that a filing exists → you fetch the filing from the exchange → the filing is the citation. If a search snippet ever becomes the thing you cite, tier 2 and tier 6 have collapsed into each other and the whole ladder above is decoration.

The failure this prevents

A search-sourced claim that gets stored as a fact does not announce itself. It sits in the index looking exactly like a filing, and it will be retrieved, cited and acted on. That is why LicenceFilterPolicy defaults scraped bodies to link_only — title and URL survive, the body never gets emitted.

T2

Filings — free in every market that matters

The highest-trust company evidence in the system, and almost none of it costs anything. What it costs is engineering.

Why the point-in-time date is the whole game

US 10-K deadlines run 60, 75 or 90 days after year end depending on filer class, and late filers exceed even that. Joining on the period a figure describes rather than the date it became public silently gives every backtest information it could not have had. One published audit found 11% of ticker-months corrupted this way. That is why FactStore stamps known_at on every fact and refuses queries that would read past it.

▪

Identity — the layer that makes "worldwide" real

Not a data source. The thing without which every source above is a separate island.

A Malay-language article says Maybank. Bursa says MAYBANK. A price feed says 1155.KL. A filing says Malayan Banking Berhad. Four strings, one company — and today link_entities resolves them by matching against a hand-written dictionary. That works for one market. It does not survive the second, and it certainly does not survive the twelfth.

SourceWhat it resolvesKeyNotes
GLEIFName → LEI. Also BIC → LEI, ISIN → LEI, and parent/child relationshipsno keyThe Global LEI Index — the closest thing to a universal company identifier, and it is public. The relationship records also give you group structure for free.
OpenFIGIISIN, CUSIP, SEDOL or ticker → FIGI, with exchange and security typefree keyFree without a key at a lower rate limit. Solves the "same company, different listing" problem that breaks naive ticker matching.
Why this is the prerequisite, not a nice-to-have

Every market added without it costs a bespoke mapping file that someone has to maintain by hand and that silently rots. With it, a new market costs an adapter and nothing else. This is the single highest-leverage piece of unbuilt work in the register.

Sanctions and entity risk

Free to download. Not free to use, in a way that is easy to miss.

The trap

OpenSanctions' terms treat screening inside a for-profit business as commercial use "even though it generates no revenue". The restriction bites on who you are, not on whether you charge for anything. Free for a personal tool; a licence purchase the moment this stops being one. The underlying government lists carry no such restriction — they are simply more work to consolidate.

▪

The legal line moved in 2026

robots.txt stopped being a courtesy and started carrying copyright weight.

Under Article 4 of the EU copyright directive, a machine-readable opt-out from text and data mining now has legal force, and the AI Act obligations that began on 2 August 2026 require respecting it. Scraping is still not illegal by default anywhere that matters — but what used to be an etiquette question is now a rights question.

Three practical consequences for this build, none of them onerous:

  • Honour robots.txt. Check it, cache it, obey it.
  • Keep the fetch log. What you fetched, from where, when, and under which rule you believed you were allowed to. This is cheap to record now and impossible to reconstruct later.
  • Default every scraped body to link_only. Title and URL survive; the body never gets emitted. The system already enforces this at retrieval rather than trusting the ingest side to remember.

Terms of service are a separate question from copyright. Breaking them rarely produces a lawsuit; it reliably produces a block, which for a pipeline you depend on is the same problem wearing different clothes.

What is wired, what is not, what I could not check

A register that overstated its own status would be the first thing in this system to do so.

Wired

GDELT 2.0, and nothing else. One source, free, keyless, worldwide. It fails loudly: a malformed response raises rather than returning an empty list, because a broken feed must never be indistinguishable from a quiet news day. An explicitly empty result — the API answered, there was nothing — is not an error and returns normally.

Not wired

Everything else on this page. The knowledge shelves are still empty and nothing else in the repository makes a network call. The point-in-time fact store is filled only by tests. That is a statement of fact, not an apology: the machinery downstream of ingest is built and tested, and each source above is now a known quantity rather than a research task.

Could not verify from this session

The network here blocks worldmonitor.app and blog.gdeltproject.org directly. Their figures above come from the project's own repository and from API write-ups rather than from the vendor documentation pages. Two specifics to confirm before building against them: WorldMonitor's public endpoint surface — the repository describes a REST gateway with rate limiting, but not which routes are stable — and GDELT's exact maxrecords ceiling, cited variously as 100 and 250. The adapter caps at 250 and pages; if the real limit is lower the API will say so and the fetch will raise rather than silently truncate.

The order to build in

1. GDELT — done. 2. Identity (GLEIF and OpenFIGI) — the prerequisite for market number three. 3. SEC EDGAR — free, keyless, and the first tier-2 evidence in the system. 4. Bursa announcements — the point-in-time date nobody sells. 5. Web search, last, because it is the one that can quietly bypass the ladder everything else depends on.

Status of these figures. Free tiers, rate limits and licence terms were checked in August 2026 and they move. Anything marked no key or free key is worth re-checking before you depend on it commercially; the licence rows are worth re-checking before you depend on them at all.
